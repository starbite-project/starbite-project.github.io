The headline

Things nobody had done before.

macOS

on a phone. Natively.

Not in a virtual machine and not on a laptop. A real macOS desktop with Dock, Finder and menu bar, booted straight on a Galaxy S9 and drawn by its Mali GPU through a Metal driver we wrote. As far as we can find, the first time anyone has done it.

LTE

through Samsung's modem, with open code.

Samsung's Shannon modem only talks to Samsung's own closed software. We booted it, served it its own calibration files, attached to the network and loaded HTTPS pages over a real SIM, from an OS we wrote ourselves.

10+

systems from one loader.

iOS, iPadOS, macOS, Windows 11, Linux, ChromiumOS, Android 17, One UI 8 and Starbite OS, all bare metal on one 2018 phone. No other device we know of boots this lineup.

The lineup

Each of these ran on the phone itself.

Nothing here is an emulator or a virtual machine. Every system boots natively from Starbite's own loader in the phone's BOOT partition.

Home screen

iOS 16 · 26 · 27, iPadOS 18

Our own XNU kernel build, device tree and drivers. CoreAnimation draws the interface on the Mali, first through OpenGL ES, later through Metal on Vulkan.

Desktop

macOS Ventura & Sequoia

Menu bar, Dock and Finder, composited on the GPU through our Metal bridge. Touch works as a trackpad, at 2x HiDPI.

Desktop + GPU

Windows 11

Our own UEFI, ACPI and drivers. A WDDM GPU driver on Mesa, all eight cores, x86 apps and Steam games, Geometry Dash at 60 fps.

Desktop + GPU

Linux · postmarketOS

Mainline kernel with Panfrost GPU, display and Wi-Fi.

Welcome screen

ChromiumOS

GPU-accelerated through Panfrost, with a working guest session.

Home screen

Android 17 · One UI 8

Both boot from the same loader, side by side with everything else.

Starbite OS Next

Our own phone OS, written from scratch.

Not Linux, not Android. Its own kernel, its own GPU compositor with a glass look, and its own drivers: Wi-Fi with WPA3, Bluetooth, camera, sound, and LTE data through Samsung's Shannon modem. Its lock screen is also the place to pick which OS to start. It runs real Linux desktop apps from its own App Store, and it has an emergency mode with a survival heater, SOS beacon and medical card.

From the lab

Stories behind it.

190 °C

The phone almost exploded

Survival Heat turns the phone into a hand warmer for real emergencies. It drives every core, the GPU and the charger at once. Normally it holds the chip at 149 °C, already far past the 125 °C its own thermometer is rated for. In one test, with the sensor's sanity check switched off, it read 190 °C. The back of the phone reached 74.5 °C, and the battery still has a hard stop.

858 MHz

A GPU 43% past Samsung's top speed

Samsung's tables stop the Mali-G72 at 598 MHz. Engineer OS, our bare-metal tuning tool, ran it at 858 MHz and read its own cycle counter to prove it. We also pushed all four big cores to 2886 MHz, past the stock 2704, until the power chip cut the whole phone off.

133,388

Instructions rewritten

iOS 26 uses a load instruction the S9's big cores have never heard of. Instead of patching programs one by one, one rule rewrote every occurrence across the entire system, all 133,388 of them, and iOS came up.

1 page

The crash that left a note

Windows died with no message. Samsung's firmware keeps one small piece of memory alive through a reset, so we made Windows write its last words there. On the next boot we read the stop code back. No more guessing.

2 kinds

Of cores, one confused emulator

x86 games crashed, but only on the big cores. Windows had saved x86 code translated on the small cores, which have different instructions, and replayed it on the big ones. A tiny driver of ours now keeps each core type's translations apart. Steam runs.

0

Help from Samsung's modem software

On boot, Samsung's modem asks the phone for its own calibration files over a private protocol. Android normally answers. We wrote the answer ourselves, and the modem came online on a real network.

Try it yourself

There is no installer. You figure it out.

⚠ Read this first

  • This is not a product, and there is no working installer. We started one and never finished it. It never fully set up a phone, and several of the systems were never part of it at all. Every OS here was brought up by hand, step by step. Getting it onto your phone means reading the code and notes, fixing paths and working out the gaps yourself.
  • You need your own copies of every OS. No Apple, Samsung, Google, Microsoft or Broadcom files are included, and we won't share them.
  • Only the Exynos Galaxy S9 (SM-G960F). Other models won't work as is.
  • It changes partitions on your phone. It can brick it. Back up everything. You use it at your own risk.
  • The project is finished and not maintained. Don't expect answers to issues.

If you still want to dig in

  1. Read the docs/ folder and the branch that covers what you want. The notes are detailed.
  2. Build the loader (shim/starlte) and the tools on a Mac or a Linux VM.
  3. Make your own OS images with the scripts under tools/, using your own original software.
  4. Expect to do it by hand. The installer branch is an unfinished experiment. Use it as notes, not as a tool.

The next chapter

Starbite was the warm-up. Starfold is the big one.

Everything we learned on a 2018 phone now goes into a brand-new 2026 flagship foldable, the Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold. Two screens, a hinge and a much faster chip mean bigger goals: every OS, done properly, on the best hardware we have ever had. The name keeps its roots: "star" from starlte, the S9's codename, where it all began.

  • Every OS again, with full graphics on our own PowerVR drivers
  • macOS and Windows with a laptop mode: half-fold it and the bottom half becomes keyboard and trackpad
  • iOS on the cover screen, iPadOS on the inner one
  • Built properly from day one: specs, tests, no shortcuts
  • Stay tuned. It will be big.
